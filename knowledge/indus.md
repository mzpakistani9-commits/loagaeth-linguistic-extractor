# Indus Valley Script — Harappan

## Corpus
- 417 signs (Mahadevan concordance, M77). 3,700+ inscriptions, average length 5.17 signs.
- Distribution: Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Dholavira, Kalibangan, Lothal, Rakhigarhi, Shortugai.
- Chronology: c. 2600–1900 BCE (Mature Harappan) with earlier pot-marks from 3300 BCE.
- Direction: predominantly right-to-left, boustrophedon on some seals.
- NO bilingual inscription found (as of 2026) — the single biggest decipherment barrier.

## Confirmed/Probable Sign Readings
- IS-342 (freq ≈ 650, ~10%) = nominative case marker -m suffix [PROBABLE, ~78% confidence]. Terminal position + Dravidian SOV pattern + affix-like behaviour.
- IS-267 (fish sign) = mīn "star" rebus (fish = star homophone in Dravidian) [PROBABLE] — Parpola 1994.
- IS-391 = divine/inaugural opener [POSSIBLE] — consistently precedes deity-following groups.
- IS-124 (jar), IS-112 (person) = [UNDECIPHERED].

## Statistical Fingerprint
- Zipf: a = 15.39, b = 2.59 — unusually steep concentration in a few signs, suggesting a constrained logosyllabic system.
- IC estimated ~0.045–0.06.
- Top 10 signs account for ~35% of occurrences.

## Dravidian Hypothesis
- Word order SOV, agglutinative morphology, post-positional suffixes, rebus principle (mīn = fish = star).
- Brahui (retroflex, agglutinative) survival in Balochistan as geographic support.
- Competing hypotheses: Indo-Aryan (Witzel), Munda/Austroasiatic (Kuiper), language isolate, and pure symbol/sherahdi system (Farrell/Knapp) — statistics contradict pure-symbol but do not decisively refute it.
